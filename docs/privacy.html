Privacy policy

What Nexus keeps, and what it never sees.

Nexus is built to browse the web without building a profile of the person browsing. This page explains, in plain language, what the app stores, what it sends over the network, and what it deliberately does not do, including the one optional home-screen banner served by Google AdMob.

Effective 31 October 2026 Android · com.nexus.browser Version 1.0

The short version

  • Nexus has no account, no analytics, and no crash-reporting SDK. It can show one small banner on the home screen, served by Google AdMob.
  • Ads never appear in the browser, in private tabs, in sheets, or in Settings. Google may use your advertising ID and ad interactions to serve and measure that banner; you can review your choices under Advertising in Settings where your region requires it.
  • Your history, shortcuts, trails, search-engine choice, and appearance choice are stored only on your device.
  • Nexus does not operate a server that receives your browsing activity. It hosts one public catalog of search presets and Today pages; the app fetches that catalog without an account, device ID, search query, or browsing history.
  • When you open a website, your device talks directly to that website, exactly as a browser does.
  • When discussion lookup is on (the default), Nexus sends the address of the page you are reading to Hacker News and Lobsters to find public threads. You can turn this off in the Discussions sheet. Private tabs never trigger this lookup.
  • Private tabs leave no history or trails, bypass WebView's page cache, and block screen capture. Android's WebView shares cookies and site storage with normal tabs, so a site may still recognize you.
  • Nothing is backed up to a cloud by Nexus. Android backup is disabled for the app.

01What Nexus stores on your device

Nexus uses Android's app-private storage. Other apps cannot read it. The data is not sent to the developer or to any third party.

Data Purpose Retention
Browsing history (page title, address, time) The Recent list and the History sheet Last 100 pages, until you clear it
Trails (groups of pages visited within about 25 minutes) The Trails shelf and the home-screen trail card Up to 30 trails, 40 pages each, until you delete them or clear history
Shortcuts (title, address) The home screen grid and the Android widget Up to 12, until you remove them
Appearance preference (light/dark) Keeps your chosen theme Until you change it or uninstall
Discussion lookup preference (on/off) Remembers whether Nexus asks Hacker News and Lobsters about the current page Until you change it or uninstall
Search choice and custom engines (name, search URL) Remembers which engine handles typed searches; engines you add stay on this device Until you change the choice, remove the engine, or uninstall
Ad consent choices (UMP) Google's consent SDK stores your advertising consent on the device so Nexus does not ask on every launch Until you change your choice, clear app storage, or uninstall
Cached catalog (search presets, Today pages, and ad settings) Lets Nexus open with the last published lists and ad configuration when the catalog endpoint is unreachable Until a newer catalog replaces it or you clear app storage
Open tabs In-memory only while the app is running Cleared when Nexus is closed
Private tab cookies and site storage Keeps sites working while you browse; Android's WebView shares one store across all tabs Until the site expires them or you clear app storage

There is no account to delete, and Nexus does not store your browsing data in the cloud. The hosted catalog database contains only public editorial and configuration data: engine names, search URLs, page titles, links, categories, notes, reading times, whether the home banner is enabled, its layout, and its ad unit IDs. It has no reader account, device ID, query, or browsing history. Private tabs write no history, trails, or page state; their cookies and site storage are not isolated from normal tabs.

How to delete it

  • History and trails: open the page menu → History → Clear history. This clears both the history list and every trail.
  • A single trail: open Trails → delete. You can rename or remove trails individually.
  • Shortcuts: press and hold a shortcut on the home screen to remove it, or use Reset in the shortcuts section.
  • Private tabs: close each tab, or use Close all private tabs in the tab sheet. This closes the pages and drops their in-memory state. Cookies set by sites remain in Android's shared WebView store until they expire; to remove them, use Clear storage below. All private tabs also disappear when Nexus closes.
  • Everything: Android Settings → Apps → Nexus → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. Because Android backup is disabled, no copy remains in a Google backup.

02What Nexus does not collect

The developer does not collect, receive, sell, or share:

  • your name, email address, phone number, or contacts;
  • your precise or approximate location;
  • your advertising ID, app set ID, or other persistent device identifiers — Google's ad SDK can access these on your device for the home-screen banner, as described under Advertising, but Nexus itself does not read or transmit them;
  • your browsing history, search terms, or a log of the pages you open (the optional, per-page discussion lookup is described under Network requests);
  • crash logs, diagnostics, or usage analytics;
  • photos, files, camera, microphone, or SMS content.

The app's merged Android manifest declares INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE for browsing and ad delivery, VIBRATE for haptic feedback, WAKE_LOCK, which the Google Mobile Ads SDK uses to keep a request alive, and com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID for advertising ID access. Camera, microphone, location, contacts, storage, notifications, and “display over other apps” permissions are explicitly removed from the Android manifest.

The catalog request does not identify you: it carries no account, device ID, search terms, or history. The hosting provider may process standard request logs such as an IP address and user agent to serve and protect the endpoint; Nexus does not use those logs to build a profile or to personalise the catalog.

03Network requests Nexus makes

Nexus is a browser, so it makes network requests as part of ordinary use.

  • Websites you open. The page is requested directly by your device from the site's server. That server receives the same information any browser would send: your IP address, the requested address, your user agent (which includes the app name and version), and cookies set by that site. The developer of Nexus does not receive a copy.
  • Search. If you type words instead of an address, Nexus opens a results page for the engine selected in Search settings. The default is DuckDuckGo (duckduckgo.com); you can choose another preset or add a custom HTTPS search URL that contains %s. Your query is sent to that engine under its own privacy policy. Nexus does not send the query to its catalog server. Search-result pages are not written to Nexus's history.
  • Catalog. Nexus fetches a small, public catalog of search presets and Today pages from a hosted HTTPS endpoint. The request is a plain read: it carries no account, device identifier, search query, or browsing history. Standard request logs at the hosting provider may include an IP address and user agent for security and reliability; Nexus does not use them to profile readers. Nexus validates the response, caches it for about an hour, and falls back to the copy bundled inside the app if the endpoint is unreachable or malformed. You can trigger a check yourself in Search settings.
  • Favicons. To show a site's own icon, Nexus requests the icon directly from that site (for example https://example.com/favicon.ico). It does not use a third-party icon service, so the list of sites you visit is not sent to an icon provider.
  • Today's page. The daily reading list comes from the same public catalog described above. Nexus does not contact a server to choose a day's page beyond that catalog fetch, and does not fetch the page itself until you tap it. If the catalog has never been downloaded, the bundled list is used.
  • Safe Browsing. Android's WebView checks addresses against Google's Safe Browsing service so it can warn you about malware and phishing. That check is performed by the Android system and may send the address to Google; Nexus does not receive, store, or transmit the result.
  • Public discussions. When “Look up discussions” is on (the default) and you open a page in a normal tab, Nexus sends that page's address to two independent public services: Hacker News through the Algolia HN Search API (hn.algolia.com) and Lobsters (lobste.rs). They return public story scores, comment counts, and comments for that address. Nexus sends no account, device identifier, search terms, or history of other pages. The answer is kept in memory for 15 minutes and is never written to history or trails. Private tabs never make this request. Turn the setting off in the Discussions sheet to stop these requests; the rest of the browser continues to work.
  • Advertising. When the published catalog enables ads, the home screen asks Google AdMob for one banner. That request goes to Google, not to the developer. See Advertising below for what it can include and how consent works.

Nexus contains no analytics SDK and no social login. The only third-party SDK that can transmit data is the Google Mobile Ads SDK described under Advertising.

04Advertising

Nexus can show one adaptive banner on the home screen, served by Google AdMob. It is the only advertising in the app, and it is off unless the published catalog enables it.

  • Where it appears. Between Today's page and Trails, or pinned above the bottom safe area, depending on the published layout. It never appears in the browser, in a private tab, in a sheet, in Settings, or on the private start page. Leaving the home screen unmounts the banner and stops its request.
  • What is requested. Nexus asks Google for one banner using a unit ID from the published catalog. The request can include the advertising ID, app set ID, IP address, device and app information, and ad interaction information such as app launches and taps. Google may use the IP address to estimate general location. Nexus does not send your history, trails, search terms, open tabs, or the address of the page you are reading to the ad SDK.
  • Consent. Before the SDK initializes, Nexus runs Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) consent flow and, on iOS, asks for App Tracking Transparency permission. If you do not consent, Nexus does not request ads. Where Google requires it, Settings → Advertising shows a Choices button that reopens the consent form so you can change or withdraw your choice at any time.
  • Your advertising ID. The Google Mobile Ads SDK can access Android's advertising ID and app set ID for ad delivery, measurement, and fraud prevention. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. Nexus itself does not read, store, or transmit it.
  • Content rating. Nexus configures the SDK with a maximum ad content rating of Teen (MaxAdContentRating.T).
  • Test mode. While test mode is on, or in any development build, Nexus requests Google's test banner instead of paid inventory.
  • Not a sale by the developer. The developer does not sell personal information. Google handles ad data under its own privacy policy: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

05Cookies and site storage

Websites may set cookies and use local storage inside Nexus's WebView, just as they do in any browser. Those records belong to the website, are stored by Android's WebView, and are not readable by the developer.

  • Third-party cookies are disabled. A page cannot use embedded content to follow you across unrelated sites.
  • First-party cookies remain enabled so that sign-ins and preferences work.
  • Android's WebView has one cookie and site-storage store for the whole app. A private tab uses that same store: cookies set in a private tab can be seen by normal tabs, and a site you are signed in to in a normal tab may already recognize you in a private tab. Closing a private tab does not clear those cookies.
  • Private tabs still write no history or trails, bypass the page cache, and skip discussion lookup.
  • Clearing history inside Nexus clears Nexus's lists. It does not clear normal-tab website cookies. To remove those, use Android Settings → Apps → Nexus → Storage → Clear storage.

06Private tabs

A private tab is a temporary browsing room. It leaves no record in Nexus, but on Android it is not a separate cookie jar.

  • No history or trails. Pages opened in a private tab are never written to Nexus's history, Recent list, or Trails. The address field hides your stored history while you are in a private tab.
  • No page cache. Private requests are made with WebView's page cache bypassed, so Nexus does not reuse saved copies of pages.
  • Cookies are shared on Android. Android's WebView keeps one cookie and site-storage store for every tab. A private tab does not isolate that store: a site can recognize you from a normal tab, signing in here can sign you in there, and closing the tab does not clear those cookies. Use Android Settings → Apps → Nexus → Storage → Clear storage to remove them.
  • No discussion lookup. Nexus does not send a private tab's address to Hacker News or Lobsters.
  • No screen capture. While a private tab is open, Nexus asks Android to block screenshots and screen recordings and to blank the app-switcher preview. On devices or builds where the native shield is unavailable, Nexus covers the app with a quiet screen instead.
  • Still a browser. A website you open still receives your IP address, the requested address, and your user agent, and can set cookies in the shared store. Private tabs do not encrypt, proxy, or anonymise traffic, and they are not a VPN or Tor.
  • Closing all private tabs closes their pages. Private tabs are held in memory only and do not survive closing Nexus. Closing them does not clear cookies or site storage, because Android shares those with normal tabs.

07Sharing into Nexus

Nexus can receive a link or plain text from another app's share sheet. The shared content is read on the device, validated, and opened or placed in the address field. It is not uploaded anywhere. If the shared item is not a web link or text, it is ignored. The share extension requests no access to photos, videos, or files.

08Android home-screen widget

The widget shows Today's page and up to three of your shortcuts. It reads them from Nexus's local storage on the device. Tapping a widget item opens Nexus through a private nexus:// link. The widget does not contain an analytics SDK and does not send data anywhere.

09Security measures

  • Only https:// pages may become the top-level document. Public http:// links are upgraded to HTTPS; cleartext traffic is disabled at the Android network layer.
  • The WebView cannot read local files (file://), and file access, file-URL access, and form-data saving are disabled.
  • Mixed content is never allowed.
  • Geolocation and camera/microphone capture are denied.
  • External app schemes are limited to mailto:, tel:, sms:, and geo:. A web page cannot launch an arbitrary Android activity.
  • Android Safe Browsing stays enabled for malware and phishing warnings.
  • Discussion lookup is limited to the address of the page being read, can be turned off at any time, and never runs in a private tab.
  • Ads initialize only after Google's UMP consent flow resolves; if consent does not permit ad requests, Nexus requests none. The banner exists only on the home screen, unmounts while you browse, and is never placed in a private tab, a sheet, or Settings. Ad content is capped at a Teen rating.
  • The catalog feed is public, read-only, and fetched over HTTPS. Nexus validates it, caches it, and falls back to the bundled copy when it is missing, malformed, or unreachable.
  • Private tabs write no history or trails, bypass the page cache, and never run discussion lookup. Android's WebView shares cookies and site storage with normal tabs; see Cookies and site storage.
  • While a private tab is open, the Android window uses FLAG_SECURE to block screenshots and screen recordings and to blank the app-switcher preview.
  • WebView metrics reporting to Google is opted out.
  • Android backup is disabled, so local browsing data is not copied to Google Drive.
  • Release builds are minified and shrunk, and WebView debugging is enabled only in development builds.

10Children

Nexus is a general-purpose browser and is not directed to children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Ad content is capped at a Teen rating, and Nexus does not ask the ad SDK to treat the app as child-directed. Because Nexus can open ordinary websites, parents and guardians should supervise use in the same way they would with any browser.
